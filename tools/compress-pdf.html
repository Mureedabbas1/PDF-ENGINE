Compress PDF: A Practical Guide

PDF files become large when they contain high-resolution scans, photographs, embedded graphics or many pages. Compressing a copy can make it easier to attach to email, upload to an application portal or store on a device with limited space. The right balance depends on the document: a photo-heavy brochure needs different settings from a contract with small text.

Choose the right compression level

Start with a lighter setting when readability matters. Use a stronger setting only when the file must meet a strict size limit. After processing, compare the original and output sizes rather than assuming the new file is smaller. If the output grows, keep the source.

Check quality before sharing

This implementation rasterizes PDF pages into images. Selectable text, search, links, form fields and vector sharpness may be lost. Zoom in on small print, signatures, tables and diagrams. Keep the original PDF as your editable and searchable master copy.

When should I compress a PDF?

Use compression for oversized scans, photo-rich documents and files that exceed an upload limit. Avoid replacing an archival or print-quality original with a compressed copy unless you have verified that the result meets your needs.

Frequently asked question

Is compression lossless? No. This tool uses image-based compression, so visual quality and text searchability can change. Always inspect the downloaded file.

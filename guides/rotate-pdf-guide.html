Rotate PDF: A Practical Guide

A page that opens sideways is one of the most common PDF annoyances. It usually comes from a scanner feeding paper the wrong way, a phone photo taken in landscape, or a landscape table dropped into a portrait report. Rotation is fast to fix, but there are a few details worth understanding so you do not rotate the wrong pages.

When to use rotate

  • A scanned batch where some sheets came through upside down or sideways.
  • A mixed document in which one wide table or drawing should be readable without turning your head.
  • A photo-based PDF that was assembled from phone pictures with inconsistent orientation.

Rotation does not change what is on the page. It tells the viewer to display the page turned by 90, 180 or 270 degrees. That is why text stays selectable and the file size barely changes, unlike tools that re-render each page.

Step by step

  1. Open the Rotate PDF tool and add your PDF.
  2. Choose the direction: 90° right (clockwise), 180° for upside down, or 90° left.
  3. In the Pages box, leave all to turn every page, or type a selection such as 2, 1-3, 7 or 9-.
  4. Click Rotate PDF. The tool reports how many pages were turned and downloads yourfile-rotated.pdf.

Working out the right angle

Picture the top of the page. If the top of the text points to the left side of your screen, the page needs to go 90 degrees right. If it points to the right, choose 90 degrees left. If the text is upside down, use 180. When unsure, rotate one page, check it, and repeat. The tool adds your turn to whatever rotation the page already has, so running it twice with 90 right gives you 180 in total, which is a frequent cause of "it got worse".

Rotating only some pages

The page selection matters most for scanned batches. Open the original, note the page numbers that are wrong, and enter exactly those. Group them by direction: all the pages that need 90 right in one run, those that need 180 in another. That is two quick passes rather than guesswork. If the wrong pages alternate in a regular pattern, for example every second page from a double-sided scan, you will need to list them individually, for example 2, 4, 6, 8.

Common mistakes

  • Rotating everything when only a few pages are wrong. Leave the default all only if all pages are wrong.
  • Confusing the printed page number with the PDF page number. Use the number your PDF viewer shows.
  • Forgetting that a page may already be flagged as rotated. Some scanners store pages upright but mark them with a rotation. Preview the result rather than reasoning from the original.
  • Expecting the file to open in the new orientation everywhere. Rotation is honoured by all modern viewers, but a handful of old tools ignore it. Test in the reader your recipient is likely to use.
  • Rotating a phone photo PDF without checking the photos. If you are building a PDF from images, see the JPG to PDF guide, which explains how to avoid sideways photos at the source.

Quality and limits

Because nothing is re-rendered, there is no quality loss. Fonts, images and vector drawings are untouched. The only limits are the usual ones: a file that needs a password to open cannot be processed, and very large files depend on your browser's available memory. The result is a new file, so the original remains unchanged on your device.

Check the result

Scroll through the whole document, not just the pages you rotated. Look for pages that were fine before and are now sideways, and check that page numbers, headers and signatures still read in the right direction. If you also plan to merge, rotate each source first, then merge, so you are not hunting for pages in a long file. Page order can be adjusted afterwards with reorder PDF pages.

A realistic example

A 12-page scan comes back with pages 3, 4 and 9 upside down and page 7 turned on its side. Run the tool twice. The first pass uses 180 degrees with the pages box set to 3-4, 9. The second uses 90 degrees right, or left depending on which way the text leans, with 7. Preview the result before sending. This two-run method is much faster than rotating everything and then fixing the pages that were correct, and it leaves untouched pages exactly as they were.

Short FAQ

Does rotating make the file bigger?

Barely. Only a small per-page setting changes.

Can I rotate by a custom angle such as 15 degrees?

No. Only 90, 180 and 270 degrees are offered. A slightly crooked scan has to be straightened in the scanning step.

Will the rotation be permanent?

It is saved in the new file. Rotating again later simply adds to it.

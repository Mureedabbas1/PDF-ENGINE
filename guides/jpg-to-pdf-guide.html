JPG to PDF: A Practical Guide

Photos of documents, receipts and handwritten notes are awkward to share as a pile of image files. Turning them into one PDF makes them easier to send, print and file. The PDF Engine converter handles the common image types and gives you control over page size, margins and order. This guide explains what to set and which pitfalls to avoid.

When converting images to PDF is the right idea

  • You photographed a multi-page document with your phone and want one file.
  • An application form asks for a single PDF, but you only have scans saved as JPG or PNG.
  • You want to send a set of receipts to an accountant as one tidy attachment.
  • You need to print several images, one per page, at a known paper size.

The tool accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP files. Note that a PDF made from photos is made of pictures; it contains no searchable text. For more on what that means, see scanned vs text PDFs.

Step by step

  1. Open the JPG to PDF tool and add your images. You can select many files at once.
  2. Arrange them in the list with the move up and move down controls. Thumbnails make it easy to check you have the right pictures in the right places. Each image becomes one page, in list order.
  3. Pick a page size: Same as image or A4 page.
  4. If you chose A4, set the margin to none, small or big.
  5. Click Convert to PDF. The tool saves images.pdf and tells you how many pages it contains and the file size.

Same as image or A4?

Same as image makes each page the shape of its picture, which suits screen viewing and photos of different proportions. Pages are sized from the image's pixel dimensions, scaled down if the picture is very large, so you may see pages of different sizes in one document. A4 page places each image on a standard sheet, centred and scaled to fit without distortion. Wider-than-tall images get a landscape A4 page, others get portrait. With a small margin (about 8 mm) or a big margin (about 17 mm), printing is safer because most printers cannot print to the very edge. Use A4 for anything that will be printed or uploaded to an office system, and Same as image for quick sharing.

Prepare the images before converting

  • Straighten and crop first. The PDF will faithfully include the table under your paper and the curtain behind it.
  • Check orientation. Phone photos can store their rotation as a hidden setting that some tools do not apply, so a picture that looks upright in your gallery may land sideways in the PDF. Check the thumbnails and, if a page is sideways, fix it afterwards with the rotate guide steps.
  • Name the files in order. Names such as page-01.jpg, page-02.jpg save you from rearranging.
  • Use good light. Even lighting and a flat page give readable results and compress better.

Common mistakes

  • Using huge originals and then being surprised by the file size. JPG and PNG images are embedded as they are, so twenty 5 MB photos produce a PDF of roughly 100 MB. Reduce the images first with the Image Compressor, or compress the finished file with the compress guide approach.
  • Converting formats that need a re-encode. WebP, GIF and BMP are converted via a PNG step, which can make them larger than the original.
  • Forgetting that transparent areas turn white or black. Check PNG logos with transparency before sending.
  • Not reading the result at full size. Zoom into small print and handwriting.

Quality and limits

JPG and PNG images are not recompressed, so what goes in is what comes out. The quality of the PDF is the quality of your photos. There is no text recognition: you cannot search or select words in the output. Very large batches use your device's memory, so for a hundred high-resolution photos, convert in two or three groups and merge the results with the merge guide.

A realistic example

You photographed six pages of a signed agreement on a desk. Straighten and crop each photo, name them page-1 to page-6, and add them in order. Choose A4 with a small margin, since the recipient may print it. After conversion, check that each page is upright and legible at 100 percent zoom. If the file is bigger than the recipient allows, reduce the photos and convert again rather than compressing the PDF, which would flatten the pages a second time.

Short FAQ

Can I mix JPG and PNG in one PDF?

Yes, any supported combination works.

Are my photos uploaded?

No, the conversion happens in your browser.

Can I turn the PDF pages back into images?

Yes, with PDF to JPG.

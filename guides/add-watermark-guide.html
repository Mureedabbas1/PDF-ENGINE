Add Watermark: A Practical Guide

A watermark is a line of text laid across the pages of a document to tell the reader something about its status: DRAFT, CONFIDENTIAL, COPY, FOR REVIEW ONLY. Used well, it prevents an unfinished version from being mistaken for the final one. Used carelessly, it makes a page hard to read or gives a false sense of security. This guide explains how the PDF Engine watermark tool works and where it fits.

When a watermark makes sense

  • Sending a draft contract or proposal for comments, so nobody signs it by mistake.
  • Sharing a copy of a document with a named purpose, such as "FOR MORTGAGE APPLICATION ONLY".
  • Marking internal material as confidential before it circulates in a team.
  • Labelling a sample or specimen page so it cannot pass as an original.

The options, in plain terms

The tool writes one line of text in the middle of every page. You choose:

  • Text, up to 40 characters. The default is CONFIDENTIAL. Only English (Latin) letters, numbers and common symbols are supported; a message in Arabic, Chinese or another script will be rejected with a clear error.
  • Size, from 10 to 200 points. The default of 60 is large and bold. Very long text at a large size will run off the page, so shorten the wording or reduce the size.
  • Angle: diagonal at 45 degrees, straight, or vertical.
  • Colour: grey, red, blue or black.
  • Opacity, from 5 to 80 percent. The default of 25 is faint enough to read through.

Step by step

  1. Open the Add Watermark tool and add your PDF.
  2. Type the watermark text. Keep it short and uppercase: one or two words read best.
  3. Pick a diagonal angle and grey colour for a classic look, or red for a strong warning.
  4. Click Add watermark, download yourfile-watermarked.pdf and inspect several pages.

Choosing settings that stay readable

The watermark is drawn on top of the existing page content, so a high opacity will cover text rather than sit behind it. Stay between 15 and 30 percent for body text. On pages with dark photographs or filled tables, grey at low opacity can disappear, and black or red may work better. If the document will be printed in black and white, check that a coloured watermark still shows as a visible grey rather than vanishing. Size matters as much as opacity: a diagonal word at size 60 on an A4 page crosses most of the sheet, which is the intent, while the same word at 150 may be cut off.

What a watermark cannot do

It is a label, not protection. Anyone who has the file can still copy the text beneath it, take screenshots, or recreate the page without it. Do not rely on a watermark to keep a document secret or to prove who leaked it, because the text is the same on every page and contains no hidden identification. For sensitive files, limit who receives them and review the checklist in PDF privacy before you share.

Common mistakes

  • Watermarking the only copy. The new file is separate, but keep your clean original for later use.
  • Watermarking before the document is finished. Assemble, reorder and number the pages first, then add the watermark last, so you never have to repeat it.
  • Using it on a document that is already watermarked. Two overlapping diagonals look messy.
  • Choosing wording that could be misread. "COPY" on a certified copy or "SAMPLE" on a live form can create confusion.
  • Letting the mark sit over a signature or a key figure. Check pages where important content is in the centre.

Quality and limits

The watermark is added as text rather than as a picture, so file size barely changes and the original text stays selectable. Every page receives the same text at the same central position; there is no tiling and no different setting per page. If you want a mark on only some pages, split those pages out, watermark them, and merge again with the merge guide steps. An encrypted file that needs a password cannot be processed.

A realistic example

You are sending a ten-page proposal to a client for comment. Choose the text DRAFT, a diagonal angle, grey colour, size 90 and opacity around 20. After downloading, open pages with dense tables and pages with photographs, because those are the places where a mark either gets lost or hides numbers. If it hides figures, lower the opacity or reduce the size. When the client approves, go back to your unwatermarked file and produce a clean final version, rather than trying to alter the draft copy.

Short FAQ

Can I use my logo as a watermark?

No. The tool supports text only.

Can the recipient remove it?

With suitable software, yes. Treat it as a visible notice.

Will it work on scanned PDFs?

Yes. The text is drawn over the scanned image. Learn more in scanned vs text PDFs.

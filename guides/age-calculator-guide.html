Age Calculator: A Practical Guide

An age calculator can work out the elapsed time between a date of birth and a chosen reference date. It is convenient for everyday planning, but official eligibility decisions should follow the rules of the organization involved.

How age is counted

Age is the number of full years that have passed since a birth date. A person turns a year older on the anniversary, not when the calendar year changes. To get a precise result you also need months and days: if the birthday has not yet happened this year, subtract one year; if the current month is earlier than the birth month, borrow twelve months; if the current day is earlier than the birth day, borrow the number of days in the previous month. The calculator performs this arithmetic so you do not have to.

Edge cases that cause mistakes

People born on 29 February have a birthday only in leap years, so different systems treat 28 February or 1 March as the legal anniversary in non-leap years. Time zones can also shift a date, which matters if you compare two people born close to midnight. When a form asks for 'age on a specific date', such as an exam, a job application or a school admission cut-off, enter that date, not today's date.

Common practical uses

Eligibility for school admission, sports categories, insurance, retirement planning, medical forms, and visa applications often depend on exact age on a given date. Genealogy and family-history projects also use age at an event, such as age at marriage. For official documents, always follow the rules and documents of the authority concerned.

More questions answered

Can I calculate age on a past or future date?

Yes. Set the comparison date to the day you need, such as an exam date or the closing date of an application.

Why do I get different answers on other sites?

Differences are usually due to how leap days, time zones or month lengths are handled. Check which date the site uses as 'today'.

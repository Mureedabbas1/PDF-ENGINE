Crop PDF: A Practical Guide

Cropping a PDF means trimming the visible area of each page. People do it to remove wide scanner margins, dark edges from photographed pages, or white borders that waste space when a page is shown on a small screen. The PDF Engine crop tool is deliberately simple, and understanding exactly what it does will keep you from using it for the wrong job.

What the tool does

The tool takes one margin value, measured in points, and trims that much from all four sides of every page. A point is 1/72 of an inch, so the default of 24 points is about one third of an inch, or just under 8.5 millimetres. The value can range from 0 to 200 points. If the margin would leave a page narrower or shorter than about 20 points, the tool stops with a message rather than creating an unusable page.

Technically, it sets the page's crop box. The page content is not erased or re-rendered; the viewer simply shows less of it. That keeps text selectable and quality unchanged, but it has a consequence covered below.

When cropping helps

  • Scans with an even blank or grey border around the sheet.
  • Presentation exports with a wide outer margin that makes content look small on a phone.
  • Documents that will be placed inside a web page or another file, where extra white space looks sloppy.
  • Preparing pages for reading on a tablet, where narrower margins mean larger text.

Step by step

  1. Open the Crop PDF tool and add your file.
  2. Enter a margin in points. Start small. Ten to twenty points trims a thin edge; 36 points removes half an inch.
  3. Click Crop margins and download yourfile-cropped.pdf.
  4. Open the result and look closely at all four edges of several pages, especially the first, last and any page with a table or footer.

Because the same value applies to every page and every side, cropping works best when the pages are uniform. If your document mixes layouts, a margin that suits a portrait page may slice into a landscape one.

Cropping is not redaction

This is the point that matters most. Content outside the new visible area is still inside the file. Anyone who knows how can reset the crop box, or extract the text, and see what was trimmed. Never use cropping to hide a name, an account number, a face or confidential figures. If something must not be seen, remove the page entirely, or recreate the document without it, and check the result as described in PDF privacy before you share.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a margin by guesswork and cutting off page numbers or footers. Page numbers are often closer to the edge than they look. Test with a small value.
  • Cropping once, then cropping the result again. The margins stack, and you can lose content without noticing. Always start from the original if the first attempt was wrong.
  • Cropping a scan that is tilted. Even margins cannot fix a crooked page. Rescan or straighten the images first.
  • Expecting a different paper size. Cropping changes what is visible, not the media size. Printing the result can still send it to the original paper size and scale it, so check print preview.

Quality and limits

There is no quality loss, since nothing is re-encoded. The file size stays close to the original, so cropping is not a way to reduce size. If size is the goal, see the compression guide. Margins in points are the only control: there is no drag-to-select box, no per-page setting and no different value for each side. For uneven margins, the safest approach is a conservative value that fits the narrowest margin in the file. Some PDF viewers show the full media box under certain settings, so the trimmed edges can reappear there.

Check the result

Page through the file at normal zoom. Pay attention to headers, footers, margin notes and any text that sits close to the edge. Then compare the page count with the original. If you plan to add page numbers after cropping, do that afterwards so they sit inside the new visible area.

A realistic example

A scanned 20-page report has a grey band about a centimetre wide down the left side of each page, where the scanner lid did not close. A centimetre is roughly 28 points. Because the tool trims all four sides equally, entering 28 will also remove a centimetre from the other edges, which is usually acceptable if the original margins were generous. Try 20 first, see whether the band is gone, then adjust. Check page numbers at the bottom edge on at least three different pages before you trust the result.

Short FAQ

Can I crop only some pages?

Not in this tool; the margin applies to every page. Split out the pages you want to treat differently, crop them, then merge again.

Why does my printed page look smaller?

Print behaviour varies between viewers. Open the print preview and choose "actual size" if you need exact dimensions.

Does cropping remove the trimmed text?

No. See the section on redaction above.
